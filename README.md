**3.1**
When I work on Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA), one of my biggest challenges is really understanding the data before jumping into the analysis. Sometimes I struggle with choosing the right charts or knowing which things are important to look at, especially when the data has missing values or outliers. Cleaning and organizing the dataset can take a lot of time, and there are moments where I feel unsure if I’m doing enough to bring out real insights.

When it comes to inferential statistics, I find it hard to decide which statistical test is actually correct for the situation. I also get confused about p-values and confidence intervals, and what they truly mean when I interpret results. There are times when I might misinterpret findings or forget to check if the assumptions of the test are met, which makes me question if my conclusions are really valid.

Regression analysis brings another level of challenge because I have to understand whether the relationships I’m seeing are meaningful or not. Interpreting coefficients, multicollinearity, and checking model assumptions can feel overwhelming. I also catch myself focusing too much on R² without fully looking into residuals to understand the reliability of the model.

For machine learning regression, shifting my mindset from explanation to prediction is tough. I sometimes struggle with choosing the right model and tuning it properly because there are many techniques like Ridge, Lasso, and Random Forest. I also need to carefully split the data for training and testing to avoid mistakes like data leakage. On top of that, I must consider whether the model is overfitting or underfitting, which adds more things to think about while improving performance.

Overall, these challenges make the learning process harder, but they also help me grow and become more confident in data analytics and machine learning. Every mistake teaches me something, and I’m gradually learning to analyze data more accurately and make better decisions based on results.
